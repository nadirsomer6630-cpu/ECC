## What the top ones share

1. **The first second asks a question.** Title card with who / when / the dilemma (#3, #11), an anomaly on a phone
   (#10), "Imagine" over an everyday situation (#1, #6, #8), or the whole premise in one breath (#4).
2. **The viewer is "tu"**, an ordinary person in an ordinary place (home, bus, flight, street, restaurant).
3. **Length**: 1:00 to 1:10 for series in parts (#3, #11, #7), up to 2:40 for single episodes (#1).
4. **One rule, tested**: wrong choices end in a loss whose clue was visible before, then a rule card (#1, #9, #10).
5. **The phone carries the story**: notifications at the top of the frame, chat bubbles, red tags (#3, #10, #9).
6. **A new shot every 1.5 to 3 s**; in dialogue, a close-up on whoever speaks (#5, #4).
7. **Labels stuck on people and objects** (stamps, name plates, posters, clue cards) (#4, #11).
8. **Emotion and humour bring shares and saves**: family (#2, #5), a perspective flip (#2), a twist (#5).
9. **Endings**: two options with what each one costs, "écris-le en commentaire", "abonne-toi pour la partie 2" (#11),
   or a precise next episode ("SÉANCE SUIVANTE : ...") (#2).
10. **Descriptions are short**: the choice as a question, the part number, two to four hashtags.

## Continuity: never confuse the viewer about who is on screen

Creator's note after nuit 4 part 1: abrupt changes between characters can confuse viewers.
- Each character keeps one colour that only they wear (courier purple, Samir green, mother pink, double black).
- A character who appears for the first time gets a name plate; a second camera (a video call) keeps the same frame
  every time it comes back (call UI, the other person's face in the corner, a name pill).
- Do not cut from one face to another face of the same size and framing without a cue: change the scale, show the
  phone, or keep the speaker's name on screen.
- A disguised character (the double wearing someone's face) must differ by one visible, constant sign (white eyes,
  the grin) and be shown in a different place from the real one.

## Method page: tesvideosvirales.com (read November 21, 2026)

A French page selling a guide and a Claude skill for animated series (17 €; only the public page was read). It claims
1.28 M views in 6 days over two Instagram series (908 k and 378 k), episodes from 90 k to 530 k. Public lessons:
close-up on the hero with one exaggerated emotion (sweat drop, wavy mouth, shock lines, heart eyes); one phrase of
3-4 words on a yellow strip at the top; three beats (hook, action, punchline); one saturated background per emotion;
characters fixed once on an emotion sheet (neutral, joy, shock, sad) and reused; a "video game" variant with gauges,
a day counter ("JOUR 12/30"), a hero sheet and a vote at the end. Applied in nuit 4 partie 1 v7 (see SKILL.md,
"One-second readability").

## Hashtags (account study, November 21, 2026)

On an account under 1 k followers, hashtags only classify the video; the first seconds decide the reach. Our 14
posts: the two best story videos carried #mystere #suspense (6.4 k and 982 views; too few to prove it). Useless or
wrong audience: #pourtoi and variants, #pov (too broad), #animation (animators), #livreur (real couriers' videos),
#thriller (film trailers), #commande #job #emploi #saturation #nuit #peur #histoires.
Rule from now on: three hashtags only, #horreur #creepypasta + one rotating test tag, same posting hour:
nuit 4 partie 1 (v7) #histoiredhorreur, next part #mystere, then #suspense; keep the winner after three posts.
Description: a line spoken by the monster as the title, the part number, the A/B question, the three tags.
